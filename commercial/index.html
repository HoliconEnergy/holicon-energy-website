OUR APPROACH

Start with the numbers and the site

Generation alone is not the full picture. The timing of electricity use and how the site imports or exports power affect the commercial case. We can assess the project scope and prepare a proposal based on relevant site information.

The commercial case should be transparent

We prefer site-specific assumptions to unrealistic headline savings. Any model should be clear about expected generation, self-consumption, electricity costs, export arrangements, maintenance and applicable commercial considerations.

  • When the business uses electricity
  • Roof suitability and electrical capacity
  • Tariffs, potential export and operational hours
  • Capital budget, maintenance and project constraints

GOOD TO KNOW

Frequently asked questions

Is commercial solar suitable for warehouses and industrial units?

Many commercial roofs can accommodate solar, but structural suitability, roof condition, access and electricity demand must be assessed before a proposal.

Do we need half-hourly electricity data?

It is particularly useful for estimating on-site solar consumption and assessing batteries. We can discuss alternative information if it is not available.

Will the installation interrupt business operations?

Some electrical work may require planned isolation. We would discuss programme, access and disruption during project planning.

Can commercial batteries improve the project?

Potentially, but the value depends on the site's demand pattern, tariff structure and battery specification. Storage should be assessed separately rather than added automatically.
